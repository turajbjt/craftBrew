If you are looking for a simple hard cider, I'd recommend that you try 
the below link.

http://www.beermanmicrobrewery.com/cider.pdf

I've made several batches of this, all with great results.


* Some Notes: In my experience, I have made minor adjustments to the 
above for better controlled & predictable results.

-- substitute commercial (filtered, pasteurized) apple juice with fresh 
(cloudier, unfiltered, less processed, pasteurized) cider from a local 
orchard whenever possible.  It makes a much better tasting hard cider.

-- boil the sugar for 10-15 minutes in some filtered water, before adding 
it to your brew.  It appears to produce better overall & predictable 
results.

-- the yeast you select makes all the difference.  I've tried this with 
several different wine, beer & champagne yeasts.  Each imparted its own 
characteristics to the batch of hard cider.  You may want to play around 
with this to find the right one for you.  I personally like the results 
achieved with Nottingham & Muntons Premium Gold dry yeasts.

-- using 4 lbs sugar, with the adjustments above, produce 5 gallon 
batches which have about 9% ABV.

-- these adjustments also cause the fermentation to take longer to 
complete [upwards of 2-3 months].  But the extra wait is worth it, when 
you taste the end results.

-- lastly, if you are one of those people who hate hazy homebrew, you can 
always use some clarifying agents to help settle the particulate.  
Personally the light hazy effect in the finished product does not bother 
me one bit.  I'm more about the overall taste & enjoying the finished 
product with my family.

Hope this helps...
